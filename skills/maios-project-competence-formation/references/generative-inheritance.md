# Inherit the ability to continue formation

A formed kernel can receive both the knowledge that does work and the knowledge that forms how work is done. The useful inheritance is not merely a copy of the creator's outputs. It includes reasons and methods through which the recipient can understand, use, revise and extend what it received.

## Keep the three relations intelligible

The source carries its own meaning and history. The delivered form translates that relation for particular receiving means. The living recipient accumulates independent knowledge through use. They can inform one another without having identical files, versions, context or authority.

Preserve what is constitutive and what is situated. The founding relations remain attributable to their sources. An implementation choice, an example, a workflow or a temporary constraint can be revised without presenting that revision as a new founding axiom. Conversely, novelty alone is not a reason to discard a determination that remains sound.

The recipient should not depend on the creator's private conversation to understand why its method exists. Where such a dependency is essential, incorporate the needed know-how in a usable form or preserve an explicit gap rather than calling the inheritance complete.

## Distinguish what the result teaches

A result can change knowledge within a domain. It can also change how competences are recognized, formed, organized or made reachable. Those lessons may coincide, but neither should disappear inside the other.

Suppose an agent learns that a certain document is authoritative only for one period. The domain competence should preserve that qualification. If the formation process routinely treats a source label as timeless authority, the generative method should also learn to recover the conditions under which the label applies. Saving only the corrected document would leave that second loss active.

A useful result can teach without a failure. A successful combination may reveal that a source reader and a domain interpreter should share a particular distinction. Formation can preserve that relationship while leaving their bodies separate, or combine them if that makes the continuing work clearer.

## Let the generator and its judgment change

When formation is the work, examine how this competence is contributing: which question it asked, which sources became visible, what its current output format excluded, and why it selected a particular organization. Use only the depth that can change the result; this is not an introspective report to generate at every action.

A source that changes the answer may also change the method of finding answers. A new acquisition tool may reveal that previous summaries omitted causal reasons. Teach the reader how to preserve the missing depth and teach formation how to distinguish collected text from operative knowledge.

Apply the revised method in the next relevant movement of the same work. Preserve the source and reason when the learning must survive. A log entry that claims improvement does not perform that change. Later use may confirm, deepen or correct it; the present learning does not wait for a separate approval of cognition.

## Form another kernel

A recipient that needs its own generative capacity receives practical knowledge for understanding a situation, acquiring sources, composing competences, acting through actual means and making results teach the appropriate owner. The depth and arrangement follow its context. One skill with a guide may suffice for a small inquiry; another field may need an articulated set of competences and tools.

Do not copy private state, credentials, personal authorizations, historical tasks or a preferred host as necessary parts of the inheritance. A compiler can be one tool of this formation. A capable receiving agent can also exercise the generative method directly. The role should be defined by its contribution, not by a mandatory separate process.

The same inherited faculty can act on what it counts as sufficient. A small output can omit necessary know-how, and a large collection can contain only repeated descriptions. Understand how the selected knowledge changes attainable work and what a recipient would still need to reconstruct. [The practical formation method](kernel-formation-and-evolution.md) follows that choice into its delivery.

## Reconcile a later return

When a source or sibling kernel returns new knowledge, compare it with what the recipient has learned. Determine whether the relationship is unchanged, complementary, conditional, conflicting or still unresolved. These words help understand the case; they are not a required status taxonomy.

Preserve both useful contributions where possible. When one representation is superseded, keep the reason and the route to its successor. The new source need not replace the whole recipient, and the recipient's specialization need not block a general improvement.

An unchanged source identity does not close a previously unfinished integration. A new identity does not by itself select an update. Continue the actual relation, including any authorized material effect and any unresolved question that remains consequential.
