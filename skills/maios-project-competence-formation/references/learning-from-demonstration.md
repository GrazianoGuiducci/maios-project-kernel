# Learn a usable method from demonstrations

A demonstration can expose knowledge that an instruction omits: a quality threshold, an exception, a recovery decision, an acceptable variation or a deliberate non-action. Learn that relationship rather than copying only the visible sequence.

A demonstration may be an operator example, a corrected agent action, paired good and bad outputs, a selected trace, a screen recording or an artifact. Use the source the current host can actually inspect. A transcript does not expose every visual detail; a screenshot does not establish an unobserved sequence.

## Recover the consequential distinction

Understand what the demonstration is trying to make possible and which parts are observed, stated by the operator or inferred. When a source already settles the intended criterion, use it. Ask for clarification only when the missing relationship would change what is learned.

Find the decisions that distinguish an acceptable result from a nearby unacceptable one. Consider variants, exceptions and recovery where the evidence makes them relevant. Do not infer a general rule from a gesture with no explanation, and do not discard an operator's stated reason merely because another theory seems plausible.

For example, an operator rewrites a reply to put an unresolved question before a proposed action. The lesson may be that the question changes whether the action is appropriate, not that every reply must begin with a question. A second case with sufficient information can preserve the same criterion by acting directly.

## Understand assistance and omissions

Attribute who supplied the key distinction or workaround. A successful continuation can include help from the operator, the model's general knowledge or a source not delivered in the package. That does not erase the success; it locates the capability that must become available to a later recipient.

A trace that reports success without the relevant intermediate evidence may support only that reported result. A source can still reveal a useful method even when its causal explanation is imprecise. Separate what happened, the proposed explanation and the proposed remedy before choosing an integration.

## Form the reusable knowledge

Teach the owning competence the distinction, its source, why it changes the work and how to recognize its next relevant use. Include an example or counterexample when it prevents mechanical replay. A one-off event can remain a contextual fact; repeated occurrence is not required when the source already establishes a reusable relation.

When the lesson changes how demonstrations are interpreted, return it to formation as well. For example, discovering that successful traces omit human interventions can change how this method attributes contributions. The domain competence and the formation competence may both learn from the same event.

Do not require a new skill, an evaluation plan and a promotion ceremony for every learned distinction. Use the existing owner when it carries the knowledge. Create a distinct competence when its own method and continuation make that useful.

## Use actual means and preserve control over effects

Observation does not authorize recording unrelated windows, publishing a trace or replaying an external action. Use the current source and the authority of the selected work. When a reproduction or comparison is useful and selected, keep its conditions and result attributable. Otherwise prepare the method without pretending that replay occurred.

Do not hard-code incidental click coordinates, local paths, provider names or temporary workarounds into a general method. Translate them through the receiving environment while keeping the reason they originally served.

## Continue the lesson

Use the learned distinction when the work next makes it relevant. Preserve meaningful knowledge in the body or references that will be read, with current continuation pointing to that depth. If later circumstances show that the correction became a new restriction, revise it through the actual source and object rather than defending it as a rule.
